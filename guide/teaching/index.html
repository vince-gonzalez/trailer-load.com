LOCK IN

How LOCK IN teaches, and how to coach from it

How LOCK IN teaches a participant, step by step, and how an instructor can read the dashboard to decide what to coach next.

1. What LOCK IN is

LOCK IN is a practice simulator for loading freight into a trailer safely: the participant builds walls of boxes, appliances, tires, bags of small packages and hazardous materials under 41 named load rules, and the simulator answers on screen with refusals, collapses, warnings and write-ups. It runs in a web browser at trailer-load.com, with nothing to install and no account for the participant.

2. What a participant practices

The 41 named rules fall into eight groups. Each rule in LOCK IN's rule list names the code that enforces it.

Skill (rules)What the participant practices
Support and stacking (7)Every box rests on something solid. Stacks taper or stay short, thin towers stay low, heavy freight gets a full floor, and an overhang never sits on an overhang.
Shelves, seams and anchors (5)Fill a row across the full width so it locks, and leave it sealed. Stagger seams like brickwork. Set special-handling freight in corners and against edges.
Balance and wall strength (2)Keep the center of gravity within 15% of the middle. Integrity drops with unsupported heavy freight, narrow towers, overhangs, voids and lean.
Density, no voids (6)Every covered empty cell costs. Fill gaps with small packages and bags, and fill the hollow of a flat tire. A wall seals at 92% height and at least 35% packed.
Fragile and crush (4)The heaviest freight never lands on an exposed fragile, small package, bag or hazmat. This-side-up boxes stay upright. Tires lie flat.
Hazardous materials (5)Hazmat loads in the bottom half of the wall, different hazard classes stay two clear cells apart, and the pairs 49 CFR 177.848 keeps apart never load close.
Workplace safety (6)Keep the aisle clear, drink water, load special-handling freight before its rack fills, clear a jammed chute, take the front piece. Warnings climb a safety ladder.
Pace (6)Steady placements build a flow streak; the rate counts freight only. In TRAILER-LOAD a wall of six or more boxes under 250 pieces an hour is flagged, and idling and re-handling are noticed.

3. How it teaches, in order

StepWhat the participant does, and why it comes here
1. Watch
TRAINING		A trainer builds one complete wall in 36 steps. NEXT moves one placement at a time; AUTO steps every 2.8 seconds until PAUSE is pressed. The first 17 placements and the closing hazmat lesson show the trainer's reason, such as why the heaviest appliance goes in a bottom corner. It ends on a nine-point training record and a sign-off. Why first: the participant sees a complete wall built, with the reasons, before handling any freight.
2. Load it with help
PRACTICE		The trainer's freight: three appliances on the rack, 32 belt pieces in the trainer's order, then a late hazmat. The loading rules apply, with no clock, score, water or write-up. A coach panel shows what the trainer said about the next piece; SHOW ME outlines the trainer's spot and says it in words. Any legal spot counts. FINISH shows a practice record: pieces loaded out of 35, pieces on the trainer's spot, how full the wall is, warnings, incidents and collapses. Why second: every decision is the participant's, on freight with a known good answer one press away.
3. Load at pace
TRAILER-LOAD		Endless walls at full tempo. A 60-second dispatch clock starts at 85% full or 85% high. A missed dispatch, five idle minutes, a starved chute or a full special-handling rack is a strike, and write-ups stop the game until signed. Why third: the same rules with the pressure and paperwork of a shift, once the participant knows what a good wall looks like.
4. The shared wall
DAILY		One wall a UTC day, built from the date, so every player gets the same freight, with a board signed in three initials. Why: a crew can load the identical wall on the same day, so people are compared on the same freight. It is a comparison for practice, not a graded test.

4. How feedback works while they load

  • Right away. A refused placement is answered with "NO SUPPORT THERE · every box needs something solid under it." Many broken rules raise a message with the fix, such as "HAZMAT TOO HIGH" with "MOVE IT DOWN TO RESTORE." Every safety warning ends with a FIX IT line.
  • The safety ladder. A warning repeated before it is fixed becomes an incident. Hazmat above the waist line, a crushed hazmat, a segregation breach, an incompatible pair too close and a second stacked tire are written up the first time; otherwise every third incident is. The fifth incident, and each one after it, loads a fresh trailer with no dispatch clock, and four write-ups end the shift. A write-up stops the game until signed and says why the rule matters. Collapses climb the same way: a banner with the fix for the first three, a warning at the fourth, an incident from the fifth.
  • The post-sort report. At the end of a run, FULL REPORT shows the last sealed wall beside the whole session: productivity against the 250 standard, safety by type, load quality and mechanics, with a grade. It prints.
  • PRACTICE keeps the rules and the messages and drops the clock and the paperwork; incidents go on the practice record.

5. What the instructor sees, and how to coach from it

The instructor makes a crew on the instructor dashboard and gives out its join code. The roster lists the least practiced (fewest walls) first and shades anyone with none. It counts TRAILER-LOAD and DAILY only: a wall when it seals, a session when the run reaches the end screen. TRAINING and PRACTICE send no walls and no sessions, and a run left through MAIN MENU sends no session.

6. A suggested first session, about 45 minutes

This is how I would run a first session. It is a suggestion, and the times are estimates.

  1. Join the crew (5 minutes). Print the crew's page from the dashboard; it lists the join steps: open trailer-load.com, choose JOIN A CREW, enter the crew code, then a name and a 4-digit PIN, or a personal code.
  2. TRAINING (10 minutes). Step through with NEXT. Before each press, ask where the next piece goes and why. Sign the training record.
  3. PRACTICE with SHOW ME (10 minutes). Choose PRACTICE on the menu. Use SHOW ME when stuck, then FINISH and read the practice record together.
  4. One TRAILER-LOAD wall (10 minutes). Seal one wall, choose NEXT TRAILER, then pause (P) and CLOCK OUT, so the session reaches the roster.
  5. The post-sort report (5 minutes). Open FULL REPORT on the end screen together. Start with safety.
  6. The roster (5 minutes). Press REFRESH on the dashboard: the session, the wall and WHAT TO COACH appear on the person's row.

7. What it is not

  • It does not certify anyone, and it does not replace forklift operator training, OSHA training, hazmat training or any certification requirement. There is no forklift in it; it is practice in loading decisions, before and alongside those courses.
  • The certificate for a clean wall, ceiling high and 95% packed, states on its face that it is a simulator achievement, not a safety certification.
  • Scores are not for deciding who is job-ready, or for hiring or placement. There are no cut scores and no reliability data behind them.
  • LOCK IN has not been studied for outcomes yet. Measuring that is what a pilot is for.

8. Data and accessibility

Data

  • No account and no email: a crew code plus a name and a 4-digit PIN, or a personal code.
  • Stored per person: the name, join and last-played dates, each sealed wall's numbers, and each session's numbers, incidents and timed move log. No field holds a disability, diagnosis or case number.
  • PINs and device tokens are kept only as one-way hashes; personal codes are kept as written.
  • Removing a person deletes their walls and sessions; deleting a crew deletes everyone on it and their results.
  • No PRACTICE record is sent; without a crew, only anonymous counts and board signatures are.

Accessibility

  • Keyboard play: arrows move a cursor, [ and ] pick the piece, R rotates, Enter or Space places. One-letter shortcuts can be turned off for speech input.
  • Colorblind modes (deutan, protan, tritan); hazmat placards carry the class number.
  • Text size: Normal, Large or X-Large.
  • Reduced motion, on from the start when the device asks for it.
  • SHOW ME's outline is also given in words for screen readers.
