THE SHORT VERSION

Playing LOCK IN is free, and you can play without an account and without telling F‑Keys who you are. F‑Keys only saves your loading stats if SAVE MY STATS is on and you are signed in. Creating an account or joining a crew turns it on, and so can signing in (see Consent). A crew sees your stats only if you joined it and chose to share with it. Your stats can also appear on the public boards below and, if you give permission, in dashboard demonstrations under a made-up name. Any player with an account can start a crew, so know whose code you are joining. Signing a wall on the arcade board is your choice too. It puts three initials and that wall's score, integrity and date on a public board, never your username. With SAVE MY STATS on, your username and best scores also appear on the public records and Smalls Sort boards, except while you are in a crew. Turning it off later does not take them off; email F‑Keys for that. You can see, export, or delete your data at any time by emailing F‑Keys. F‑Keys does not sell personal data.

Who Runs LOCK IN

LOCK IN is a freight-loading training simulator operated by F‑Keys Creative LLC ("F‑Keys"), a Florida limited liability company, and made available at www.trailer-load.com. This Policy explains what information the game and this website collect, how F‑Keys uses it, and the choices you have. It applies to the LOCK IN game, this website, and the program dashboard used by crews, schools and employers.

What Is Collected

Gameplay data · only if you opt in

When SAVE MY STATS is on and you are signed in, the game saves information about how you load trailers: pieces placed and their positions, rotations, shelves locked, recoveries, collapses, hazmat handling, water/hydration breaks, tire-nesting actions, lifts, rejected drops, safety warnings, incidents and in-game write-ups (with the rule broken), boxes you staged, picked up again, or left on the dock, density, the game mode, score, pace (pieces per hour), wall integrity, session length, and a per-session summary of these figures. Each saved session also carries a timed log of your moves (up to 6,000 per session, timed to a tenth of a second), its start and end times, the app version, your two switch settings, the crew code on your device if there is one, and an ID made on your device from the time it was made and a random part. Pressing SIGN OUT deletes that ID from the device, and the game then makes a new one. Each sealed wall, each daily score, and each Smalls Sort run is saved too. This data is about how you play, and it is saved to your account.

Account information · only if you create an account

If you create an account to keep your record across devices, F‑Keys collects a username you choose and a password (stored only as a bcrypt hash; it is never stored or displayed in readable form). No email address is required, and the game does not ask a player for one or send one with your sessions. Sign-in uses an internal address made from your username. No mail is sent to it. Earlier versions sent an email address with each saved session: one typed into an optional box, or an address made from your username. F‑Keys has deleted these. Every account gets a recovery code. With your username, it resets your password if you forget it. Keep it private: anyone with your username and the code can set a new password. The game shows you the code once, when you create your account. Before October 16, 2026, an account made with the profile's "Create a free account" button was not shown its code. F‑Keys may also ask for the code to confirm a request is yours, and keeps only a scrambled (hashed) copy. The device you made the account on also keeps the code, and pressing SIGN OUT there deletes it. If F‑Keys issues a facility login with a license, it uses the instructor's email address. Creating an account is optional and free.

Crews · only if you start or join one

Any player with an account can start a crew and give out its join code. If you start one, F‑Keys keeps its name, its join code, and your account as the one who runs it. A free crew holds 9 members plus the player who runs it; a licensed crew has no limit. If you join one, after a confirmation card that names the crew and lists the main things it will see, the code is stored with your account so whoever runs the crew sees your username and the results you share (see Sharing). Leaving the crew removes the code from your account. Sessions you saved while in it keep the code they were saved with, and the crew can no longer see them. Some older versions also saved sessions played without an account, under the random device ID. F‑Keys deleted them on October 16, 2026.

Arcade board initials · only if you sign a wall

After you finish a wall you can sign it with three initials. No account is needed. F‑Keys keeps the initials, the mode, that wall's score, integrity, pieces and pack, and the date and exact time you signed. If you are signed in with SAVE MY STATS on and not in a crew, it also keeps which account signed. Your profile lists those initials under "Your initials on the board", and while you are signed in the board marks them as yours. Only you see that link. No other player, crew, or instructor can. As a guest, while you are in a crew, or with SAVE MY STATS off, nothing else is kept with your initials: no account and no device. Pick letters that do not identify you. If the same wall is also saved to your account, anyone who can see it under your username, on a records board or through a crew you share with, could match the numbers.

Device & technical data

The database and sign-in provider (Supabase), the website host (GitHub Pages), and the content-delivery and security provider (Cloudflare) may process limited technical data such as IP address, browser type, approximate region, and pages requested, as an ordinary part of serving and securing the site, and may keep it in their service logs. When you sign in, the sign-in system also stores that sign-in's IP address and browser type with your account in the LOCK IN database. Pressing SIGN OUT asks it to end that sign-in, which removes that record. That request can fail, for example when the sign-in has already timed out. A sign-in that is not ended this way can stay on file until the account is deleted. The account also keeps the time of its latest sign-in until it is deleted. Cloudflare also sends Network Error Logging headers, which ask your browser to report failed page loads to Cloudflare. F‑Keys adds no analytics or tracking script of its own. The only counting F‑Keys does is one visit total and a set of anonymous daily totals, both described in Cookies & Analytics.

F‑Keys does not collect: a student email or phone number (accounts use a username only), your real name, location beyond approximate region, payment card numbers (handled by the payment processor for facility purchases, never by F‑Keys), or any biometric data.

Consent & Your Choices · Two Switches

There are two switches. Crew sharing needs both, and a crew's confirmation card turns both on when you join.

  • SAVE MY STATS. The box reads "Save my loading stats (my record)." Off by default. Creating an account turns it on, because keeping your record is what an account is for. Joining a crew turns it on too. With it on and signed in, your sessions, sealed walls, daily scores and Smalls Sort runs are saved to your account, and walls you sign are linked to it unless you are in a crew. With it off, or as a guest, none of that is saved. Your device keeps your personal bests and daily streak either way.
  • The sharing box. It reads "Share my stats with my crew." Off by default, and it works only with SAVE MY STATS on. Joining a crew turns it on, after a confirmation card that names the crew and lists what it will see. Whoever runs the crew then sees your username with the sessions and walls you share.

Both boxes are in ADMIN > ACCOUNT & DATA (pause the game to open ADMIN), and you can change either at any time. While you are in a crew, it sees every session you saved with the sharing box on, including sessions from before you joined it and sessions you shared with an earlier crew. Your walls follow your most recent saved session: while that session has sharing on, the crew sees every wall saved to your account since you joined. Turning sharing off keeps new sessions from the crew, and once a session is saved with sharing off, the crew stops seeing your walls. Sessions you already shared stay visible until you leave the crew. Leaving the crew hides everything from it.

Signing in and shared computers. Signing in turns SAVE MY STATS on only if no choice has been saved on that device. If it was saved off there, it stays off, and the sign-in message says whether it is on or off. The choice is kept on the device, not on your account, and signing out keeps it. Pressing SIGN OUT turns sharing off on that device, but you stay in your crew; turn the sharing box back on to keep sharing. On a shared computer, look at both boxes after you sign in to see whether each is on, and press SIGN OUT when you finish. Until you do, the next person on that browser can be signed in as you. Apart from a wall you sign, the only gameplay data that leaves your device with SAVE MY STATS off is the anonymous daily totals described in Cookies & Analytics.

Students & Minors

Read this if the player is under 18. LOCK IN is offered to vocational and career-technical-education (CTE) programs, trade schools, and employers. Some players may be minors.

Under 13. LOCK IN is not directed to children under 13, and F‑Keys does not knowingly collect personal information (such as a username or saved stats) from children under 13. Creating an account does not ask your age. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, contact F‑Keys and it will be deleted. Children under 13 may still play as guests. If they sign a wall, F‑Keys keeps only what any guest signature keeps: the initials, the mode, that wall's numbers, and the date and time.

Ages 13-17 (students). Where LOCK IN is used inside a school or CTE program, the school may act as the party that provides notice to, and obtains consent from, students and their parents or guardians under applicable law, including the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) and state student-data-privacy laws. Where a school uses LOCK IN under a signed agreement with F‑Keys, that agreement can name F‑Keys a school official under FERPA, and it controls how F‑Keys handles that school's student records. F‑Keys uses student gameplay data only to run and improve LOCK IN. The exceptions are the public boards, which show a student's username and best scores once the student saves records with SAVE MY STATS on, but never while the student is in a crew (see Sharing), and a dashboard demonstration (see How Data Is Used), which includes a player in a school or training program only with that program's written agreement. F‑Keys does not use student personal information to serve targeted advertising and does not sell student data.

Schools and educators: if you intend to deploy LOCK IN with minors, contact F‑Keys first so the appropriate data-protection terms can be put in place. F‑Keys recommends you obtain parent/guardian consent consistent with your institution's policies before students create accounts or share data.

How Data Is Used

  • To run the game and show you your own stats and rating.
  • To keep your record across devices (if you have an account).
  • To show your crew the sessions and walls you have chosen to share, in the game and, for a licensed crew, on the program dashboard.
  • To understand, in aggregate and de-identified form, how the training is used so F‑Keys can improve it, including the anonymous daily totals described below.
  • With your permission only: to show your gameplay statistics in demonstrations of the dashboard to schools and employers, under a pseudonym such as student-7f3, never your username. The numbers are real, so someone who compares them with the public boards could tell whose they are. F‑Keys includes a player only after they give permission, and records it on their account. For a player in a school or training program, F‑Keys also needs that program's written agreement. You can withdraw at any time by emailing F‑Keys.
  • To keep the service secure and prevent abuse.

F‑Keys does not sell personal information, and does not use saved gameplay data to target advertising to you.

Sharing

F‑Keys shares personal information only in these limited cases:

  • With the payment processor and the email provider · when a facility pays by card, and for email you send F‑Keys.
  • On the public leaderboards · once you save records with SAVE MY STATS on, your chosen username and figures from your saved records can appear on the public records boards (best session score, total score and walls loaded, fastest wall, cleanest wall, and fewest pieces) and on the Smalls Sort board (score, accuracy, sort rate and pieces sorted). Any signed-in player can follow a username, which also tells them whether that username exists, and their friends board then shows that player's username, best score, and the most walls they loaded in one session. F‑Keys stores who you follow, and only you can see that list. The player you follow is not told. The daily and arcade boards show initials only. Usernames need not be your real name. While you are in a crew, your username is left off the public records and Smalls Sort boards and off other players' friends boards. You can still sign walls on the arcade board, and a wall you sign while in a crew is never linked to your account. If you leave the crew, records you saved can appear on those boards again. To stay off those boards outside a crew, play with SAVE MY STATS off. Turning it off later stops new records, but records already saved stay on the boards until you email F‑Keys and they are deleted. Deleting them also removes them from your crew and your profile. Records saved later with SAVE MY STATS on go on the boards again.
  • On the arcade board · if you sign a wall, your three initials and that wall's score, integrity and date are public. The board never shows a username. It shows which account signed only to that account, by marking its own initials as yours.
  • With your crew · only the crew you joined, and only what you share with it (see Consent for which sessions and walls that covers). Whoever runs the crew sees your username, the dates you made your account and joined the crew, and, for your shared sessions and walls, figures such as score, pace, integrity, density, safety incidents, collapses, water breaks, rotations, lifts, when you last played, and the app version. They see this in the game. If the crew has a license, they also see it on the program dashboard and can export it to a CSV file. Through the LOCK IN data API they can also read your account ID, your account flags (one says whether you gave permission to use your stats in demonstrations), and each shared session's full saved record, including the timed move log, the random device ID, and the crew code saved with it. A session you shared with an earlier crew carries that crew's join code. An exported file leaves LOCK IN; deleting your data with F‑Keys does not delete their copy.
  • In demonstrations, with your permission · under a pseudonym, never your username, as described above.
  • With service providers who run the infrastructure: Supabase for the database and sign-in, GitHub Pages for this website, and Cloudflare for content delivery and security.
  • For legal reasons · if required by law, or to protect rights, safety, or the integrity of the service.
  • In a business transfer · if the Service or its operator is involved in a merger, acquisition, or asset sale, with notice to you.

Cookies & Analytics

F‑Keys does not use Google Analytics and adds no analytics, advertising or tracking script of its own. There is no advertising and no ad technology of any kind on this site. The only cookie-like storage LOCK IN uses is your browser's local storage and session storage, both on your device. Local storage keeps your settings, your personal bests and daily streak, your SAVE MY STATS choice and sharing setting, a random device ID, the last initials you signed with, and a few flags: whether you finished the briefing, whether you have played, whether you chose to play as a guest, whether a SAVE MY STATS choice was saved, and how many times that device signed out. If you typed an email address into an older version, your device also keeps it until you sign in or press SIGN OUT. Once you sign in, it also keeps your sign-in and your username. If you join a crew on that device, it keeps the crew code. If you created your account on that device, it keeps your recovery code. Pressing SIGN OUT on any LOCK IN page (the game, your profile, Smalls Sort, or the program dashboard) signs you out of all of them on that device, in every open tab, and asks the server to end that sign-in. It also clears the remembered initials, the username, the recovery code, the crew code and sharing setting, and the device ID. Your SAVE MY STATS choice stays. When a sign-in expires instead, your username, crew code, sharing setting, recovery code and device ID stay on the device until you sign in again and press SIGN OUT, or clear your browser. The front page also keeps one aggregate total, visits. It is a single integer on the LOCK IN server. Your browser adds 1 to it once per tab session, and one flag is stored in your browser's sessionStorage (cleared when you close the tab, never transmitted) so that a page refresh does not double-count. The game keeps a few other tab-only flags there too, such as your level bests for that tab. No row, cookie, or fingerprint is recorded for a visit, and the total holds no IP address. The database provider's request log records each request, including this one, with its IP address, browser type, approximate location and network, and a technical fingerprint of the connection (see Device & technical data). The total contains no personal data.

The game also keeps a few more daily totals: how many times it was opened, how many games were started in each mode, how many walls were finished in the daily and shift modes, how many daily walls were submitted, how many times the briefing was finished, and how many walls were signed on the arcade board. Each is one number per day. Nothing about you is attached to a total: no account, device, cookie, or IP address. As with a visit, the database provider's request log records each request with its IP address, browser type, approximate location and a technical fingerprint of the connection. Whether a visit counts as new or returning is decided on your own device, which keeps a single "1" in local storage to remember it has been here; only which total to add 1 to is sent. These totals are private and are used only to see what in the game is working.

Data Retention

F‑Keys keeps the session, wall and daily records saved while SAVE MY STATS was on until you, your school or your program asks F‑Keys to delete them, or until the account is deleted for inactivity. Turning SAVE MY STATS off does not delete them. An account with no sign-in and nothing saved or signed for 24 months is deleted, with everything saved to it. The only accounts kept longer are the F‑Keys demonstration accounts and the account that runs a licensed crew while it is licensed. F‑Keys acts on a confirmed request to delete within 30 days. When a school's or program's agreement with F‑Keys ends, F‑Keys deletes the student records it covers within 60 days, unless the agreement says otherwise. The game no longer makes copies of your sessions on your device. Copies older versions made are deleted the next time you finish a session with SAVE MY STATS on, the next time you press SIGN OUT, or when you clear your browser. Settings, personal bests and your streak stay in your browser until you clear them. Records you shared with a crew can stay visible to it until you leave the crew; for a licensed crew, its agreement with F‑Keys also applies. F‑Keys keeps signed initials and their numbers. They show on that day's board and can show on the all-time board. If your account is deleted, the link between your signatures and your account is removed, and the initials stay. A crew you started keeps its name, its join code and its members, and no one can see the members' results through it.

Security

Your saved data is stored on a managed database with row-level security. Other players cannot read your saved records; they see only what the public boards show (see Sharing). Whoever runs a crew you joined can read your username, your account ID, the dates you made your account and joined the crew, your account flags (one says whether you gave permission to use your stats in demonstrations), and the results you shared with it, as Sharing describes. The list of walls you signed can be read only by your own signed-in account, and crews cannot see it. Passwords are stored only as bcrypt hashes; they are never stored or displayed in readable form. No system is perfectly secure, but F‑Keys works to protect your information and to limit what is collected in the first place. If F‑Keys confirms that saved data was accessed without permission, it will tell each school, program and employer that has an agreement with it and whose players are affected, as soon as it confirms it and within 72 hours, and will post a notice in the game.

Your Rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal information, to withdraw consent, and to object to certain processing. To exercise any of these, email F‑Keys (below). Player accounts have no real email address, so before F‑Keys acts on a request for a username it confirms the account is yours: include your recovery code. If you have no recovery code, send the request through your school or training program; that is the only other way F‑Keys can confirm an account is yours. F‑Keys acts on a confirmed request within 30 days. You can also turn SAVE MY STATS or the sharing box off at any time in ADMIN > ACCOUNT & DATA, and you can delete locally stored data by clearing your browser. Turning SAVE MY STATS off stops new links between walls you sign and your account. It does not remove links already made. It also does not delete sessions already saved or take you off the public boards. To have those removed, email F‑Keys. F‑Keys will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

International Users

LOCK IN is operated from the United States, and saved records are stored in the United States. If you use it from outside the U.S., you understand your information may be processed in the U.S. Where required (for example, for users in the EU/UK), F‑Keys processes data on the lawful bases of consent and legitimate interest in providing the service.
