DAY ONE · THE BRIEFING

HOW TO BUILD
A FREIGHT WALL.

Every rule the game enforces, in the order you meet them. This is what a loader learns in their first week on a dock, written down, which is not something that normally happens.

STRIP 01

The bay

Seventeen cells wide, twenty tall. Row zero is the ceiling.

Three hundred and forty cells. You are filling a wall from the floor up, and the wall seals once it reaches the top. Everything below is measured from the ceiling down, so when a rule says a piece must ride low, it means a high row number.

STRIP 02

Where freight comes from

Two. The belt, and the staging tray beside it.

SourceWhat arrivesHow it behaves
The belt10 visibleBoxes, and the occasional bag of smalls. The front piece is free to take.
Staging3 piecesTwo tires and one hazmat. They are there from the start and they are the only pieces that earn anchor bonuses.
THE SAME THREE, FOR EVERYBODY

Staging is set from the date, so every player on Earth opens the day with the same two tires and the same hazmat. Nothing else arrives in staging during the wall. What you see at the start is the whole supply, and planning around it is most of the job.

Taking from the back costs you

Reaching past the front of the belt to grab something you like better is charged per piece you reach through. Cherry-picking is a real habit on a real dock and it is a real drag on throughput.

STRIP 03

Support

Three quarters of it needs something underneath. Or: both shoulders braced and one foot down.

That is the whole rule, and it has two halves. Most people find the first half in their first minute and never find the second.

The second half, exactly. A piece with less than three quarters underneath still goes down if all three of these are true at the same time: something is touching it on the left, something is touching it on the right, and at least one of its bottom columns is resting on something. It is not floating. It is bridging, with one point of contact below and pressure on both sides.

WHY IT WORKS THIS WAY

A box braced hard on both sides is held by friction and pressure, not by what is under it. Loaders use this constantly to close a gap that nothing fits cleanly into. The game models it because the building does.

STRIP 04

The shelf

Fill all seventeen cells of a row and it locks. This is the whole game.

A locked shelf is not just points. It changes the physics of everything above it.

  • It heals the wall's integrity on the spot.
  • It sets a floor the wall can no longer fall through.
  • It protects everything above it from being crushed, permanently.
  • It reduces the penalty carried by every piece sitting on top of it.

Stagger your courses

Three or more same-width pieces stacked in a perfect vertical column make a fault line straight up the wall, and it is penalised. Offset each course sideways from the one under it, the way a bricklayer does. Four staggered courses earns a brick lock.

STRIP 05

Fragile

You have been told fragile rides on top. That is incomplete, and it is why people lose fragiles on row three.

Here is what actually happens. Only the heaviest class of freight can crush anything at all. A mid-weight crate landing on a fragile does nothing. And a fragile is safe, at any height, if any one of these is true:

  • A full shelf is locked anywhere below it. Lock one row and everything above it is protected for the rest of the wall.
  • Its own row is a complete shelf.
  • It has a real box on its left and a real box on its right.
THE TRAP

The trailer wall is not a box. A fragile pressed against the left or right wall can never earn side protection, because there is nothing on one side to brace it. The corners are the dangerous place, not the safe one.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD

You do not need height. You need a closed course. Build fragiles into the middle of a row, put something flush on each side, and finish the row. A bag of smalls counts as crushable too, so the same rule protects your customers' packages.

STRIP 06

Hazmat

Dangerous goods load low, stay apart, and get capped. Three rules, three consequences.

RuleWhat it means
HeightBelow the waist line, which is the bottom half of the bay. Any part above it is a violation.
SegregationTwo different hazard classes need two clear cells between them.
Killer pairsSome class combinations cannot be near each other at all.

Height and segregation both cut your score hard, and both are checked live. Move the piece and the score comes back. That is deliberate: the point is to teach the correction, not to punish one misclick.

NO SECOND CHANCE ON THIS ONE

Crushing a hazmat is written up the first time it happens. Every other mistake in the game needs a pattern before it reaches paper. This one does not, because it is a hurt-somebody event.

STRIP 07

The six ways it comes down

Every collapse in the game is one of these six. Learn to recognise them and you stop having them.

1 · No support

Refused outright. Nothing breaks, you just lose the moment. This is the cheap one.

2 · A tire on its edge

A tire standing on edge rolls. Anything placed one row above it topples. Lay tires flat, every time.

3 · Heavy with no base

Heavy freight needs something solid under every cell, not three quarters. It comes down, and it takes whatever it was crushing with it.

4 · Column integrity

A narrow pillar only carries so much above it. Go past the limit and the column snaps.

5 · The staircase

An overhang stacked on another overhang. Two hangs in a row and the packages come down. Build a full base or flag for a piece that lines up.

6 · The freestanding stack

Same width, three high, nothing bracing it: it goes over. Any stack five high goes over regardless. A strict taper, each layer narrower than the one below, is the exception.

STRIP 08

The veteran moves

The things nobody writes down, which is the reason this page exists.

Nest smalls in a tire

A tire laid flat has a hollow in the middle. That hollow is dead air the belt can never fill. Drop a bag of smalls into it.

Pack the roof

The gap between the top of your wall and the trailer ceiling is wasted cube. Bags of smalls go up there, and five of them make a full ceiling course.

BAGS ARE SCARCE, SO SPEND THEM WELL

Bags arrive on the belt and they are not common. Between nesting one in a tire and packing one against the roof, you will rarely have enough for both. Flagging a coworker is the one way to get another: it takes the piece you cannot use and puts a bag on the belt in its place.

Anchor your non-conveyables

Appliances, furniture and tires do not stack like boxes, so put them where the trailer itself braces them. The first one you place anchors the whole load. One touching two edges at once is better still.

Flagging for help

You can wave a coworker over to take a piece you cannot use. The first few are free. After that it costs more each time, and it climbs fast. On a real dock the same thing is true, it just costs you differently.

STRIP 09

Pace

Two hundred and fifty pieces an hour. That is the hub standard, and it counts freight only.

Smalls do not count toward your rate. Neither do bags. Stopping to break down a bag of smalls to chink your gaps is time you are not moving freight, and the clock keeps running while you do it.

THIS IS NOT A GAME RULE

It works this way on the floor for the same reason. A supervisor counts pieces, and a bag of smalls is one piece whether you place it whole or spend four minutes distributing it.

Flow

Clean placements in a row build a streak that pays more the longer it runs. A crush or a collapse resets it to nothing. Steady beats frantic.

STRIP 10

The record

The game separates a mistake from a pattern, because a workplace does.

  • One broken box is a mistake. It costs you, it breaks your streak, and that is the end of it. It is not a safety event.
  • A third broken box starts the ladder. Eyes go on you when the trailer keeps having damage, not when it has some.
  • A warning needs a repeat to become an incident. Paper lands on a pattern.
  • You can wash a warning by locking a shelf, drinking, or flagging for help. The record still shows it was washed.

Dispatch

Once the wall is nearly full, the truck is waiting. Seal it inside the window and you are paid for it. Miss it and the trailer leaves without a full wall.

The certificate

The hardest thing in the game, and it is deliberately hard. It needs a wall to the ceiling, packed tight, at full integrity, with zero collapses and zero incidents. Not a good wall. A perfect shift.
