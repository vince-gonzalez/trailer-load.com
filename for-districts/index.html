THE SHORT VERSION

Nothing to install. No ads. No third-party trackers. No student email required. LOCK IN, made by F-Keys Creative LLC ("F-Keys"), runs entirely in a web browser, saves a student's stats to an account only while SAVE MY STATS is on, keeps everything first-party, and can be checked in your browser's developer tools. This page is the reference for your technology, privacy, and purchasing reviewers.

Overview

LOCK IN is a browser-based freight and trailer-loading training simulator for career and technical education. Students build trailer walls under a dispatch clock and are held to real load theory: support, bracing, staggered seams, weight balance, hazmat placed low, fragile freight protected, void elimination, and a graduated safety ladder. The game is free for individual players forever. Any player with an account can start a free crew of up to 9 members and see their shared results in the game. A license adds the instructor program dashboard, which shows opted-in students' practice results and exports them to CSV, along with facility logins, a signed data agreement, purchase orders and invoices, support, and no member limit.

Delivery model

  • No installer. No plugin, no browser extension, no app-store package, no MSI, no agent, no administrator rights.
  • No on-premise component. Nothing runs on district infrastructure; no local service and no listening port on the device.
  • A student opens a URL. All gameplay code runs client-side in the browser. Works on current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari, including Chromebooks. Designed for keyboard and mouse on classroom desktops and laptops.

Security & Privacy

  • No advertising and no ad technology of any kind, anywhere on the domain. Advertising was removed permanently and is barred.
  • No Google Analytics, and F-Keys adds no analytics or tracking script of its own. No tag manager, no advertising pixels, no third-party session-replay script. A reviewer can confirm this in browser developer tools. The game's own timed move log is described under Data Handling. Cloudflare, the content-delivery provider, sends Network Error Logging headers, which ask the browser to report failed page loads to Cloudflare.
  • First-party data only. Gameplay records are stored in the LOCK IN database (Supabase / PostgreSQL, hosted in the United States) with row-level security. A signed-in student can read only their own records, plus, if they start a crew, its members' usernames, account IDs and account flags (one says whether the member allowed dashboard demonstrations), the dates they made their accounts and joined the crew, and the results they share with it. Other users see only the public boards described below.
  • Opt-in, off by default. SAVE MY STATS (the box "Save my loading stats (my record)." in ADMIN > ACCOUNT & DATA) is off by default. Creating an account or joining a crew turns it on. Signing in turns it on only on a device where no choice was saved; a saved OFF stays off. The setting is kept on the device, not the account, so on a shared computer the student should look at it after signing in and press SIGN OUT when finished. With it off, or as a guest, no session, wall or score is saved for the student, and a wall the student signs keeps only the initials, the mode, that wall's numbers, and the date and time, with no account or device attached. The only other gameplay data sent is a set of anonymous daily totals, such as walls finished per mode: one number per day, with no account, device, cookie or IP attached. The database provider's request log still records each request with its IP address, browser type, approximate location and a technical fingerprint of the connection.
  • Two switches for instructor visibility. A crew sees a student's results only while the student is in it. It sees every session the student saved with SAVE MY STATS and the sharing box ("Share my stats with my crew.") both on, including sessions from before the student joined and sessions shared with an earlier crew. It sees walls saved since the student joined, while the student's most recent saved session has sharing on. A crew owner can also read each shared session's full saved record through the LOCK IN data API, including the timed move log, the random device ID, and the crew code saved with it, which for a session shared with an earlier crew is that crew's join code. Joining a crew, after a confirmation card that names it and lists the main things it will see, turns both switches on. Pressing SIGN OUT turns the sharing box off on that device, and signing in again does not turn it back on. The student stays in the crew and turns sharing back on in ADMIN > ACCOUNT & DATA. Any student with an account can start a crew. The dashboard does not show arcade signatures, and a wall a student signs while in a crew is never linked to the student's account. A signed wall's score, integrity and date are public, so anyone who can see that wall under the student's username, through a crew it was shared with or a records board, could match the numbers.
  • All game data goes to the LOCK IN database over HTTPS/TLS (TCP 443, outbound). No inbound connections to the device.
  • Notice of unauthorized access. If F-Keys confirms that saved data was accessed without permission, it tells each affected school, program and employer with an agreement as soon as it confirms it and within 72 hours, and posts a notice in the game.

Data Handling

What an account collects

  • A username, a password (stored hashed), and a recovery code (stored hashed). The account also keeps the date it was made, the crew code and join date if the student joins a crew, whether the student gave F-Keys permission to use their stats in dashboard demonstrations under a made-up name, and the usernames the student follows. The sign-in system also keeps an internal address made from the username, to which no mail is sent, and the IP address, browser type and time of each sign-in. SIGN OUT asks the server to end that sign-in, which removes that record. That request can fail, for example when the sign-in has already timed out, and then the record can stay until the account is deleted. The account also keeps the time of its latest sign-in until it is deleted.
  • No email address required. The game does not ask a student for one or send one with sessions. No real name, no phone, no home address, no date of birth, and no payment data from students. If F-Keys issues an instructor login for a paid dashboard, it uses the instructor's email.
  • If the student opts in: gameplay metrics (score, load integrity, density, pace, safety incidents, collapses, water breaks, rotations, lifts, walls completed); a timed log of moves (up to 6,000 per session, at 0.1 s); session start and end times; app version; a random device ID (pressing SIGN OUT deletes it from the device); the crew code on the device and the two switch settings; per-wall records, daily scores and Smalls Sort runs; and, for walls the student signs while signed in and not in a crew, a link to the student's account that only the student can see.

Where it lives and who touches it

  • Subprocessors for the product, complete list: Supabase (database and authentication hosting, United States region us-east-2; it stores all gameplay and account data), GitHub (GitHub Pages, website hosting), and Cloudflare (content delivery and security for the website). F-Keys uses no others. Each provider uses its own subprocessors. The mail provider (for email sent to F-Keys) and the payment processor (for facility card payments) are not part of the product and receive no data from it.
  • Export: a licensed program can export its roster's data as CSV from the program dashboard at any time. A free crew sees its members' shared results in the game, and its owner can read the shared records through the LOCK IN data API.
  • Return and deletion on termination are addressed in the data privacy agreement (below).
  • Retention and deletion: saved records stay until the student, the school or the program asks F-Keys to delete them, and F-Keys acts on a request within 30 days. An account with no activity for 24 months is deleted with everything saved to it. When a school's or program's agreement with F-Keys ends, F-Keys deletes the student records it covers within 60 days, unless the agreement says otherwise. A student without a recovery code makes a request through the school or program. Send requests to privacy@trailer-load.com. A CSV a crew exported is outside LOCK IN, and deleting with F-Keys does not delete it.
  • Public leaderboards: for students who are not in a crew and saved records with SAVE MY STATS on, even if they turn it off later, the records boards and the Smalls Sort board show a chosen username with figures from that student's saved records (best session score, total score and walls loaded, wall time, integrity or piece count, and Smalls Sort accuracy and rate). Any signed-in player can follow a username and see that player's best score and most walls loaded in one session on a friends board. A student in a crew is left off the records, Smalls Sort and friends boards while in it, and can still sign walls on the arcade board; if the student leaves the crew, saved records can appear on those boards again. The student or the school can ask F-Keys to delete those records, which also removes them from the crew. Records saved later with SAVE MY STATS on appear again. The daily and arcade boards show three self-chosen initials with that wall's score, integrity and date, never a username or account. Usernames and initials are user-selected and need not identify the student.

Accessibility

Implemented and shipping today: keyboard play for every menu and game mode (Tab and Enter in the menus; arrows move, [ and ] choose a piece, Enter or Space places, R rotates, P or Escape pauses; one-key shortcuts can be turned off in Settings), colorblind modes, a reduced-motion mode, in-game text scaling, and high-contrast outlined text for mid-game readability. This page describes what is built rather than claim a formal conformance level: LOCK IN has not undergone a third-party WCAG audit and F-Keys does not assert a WCAG or Section 508 conformance level. If your institution requires a formal accessibility representation as a condition of award, contact F-Keys to discuss scope.

Content & Age Suitability

  • Workplace-safety and logistics themed. No violence, no graphic content.
  • No student-to-student chat and no free-text messaging between users. The only user-authored text visible to others is a self-chosen username on the leaderboards, three initials on the arcade board, and a crew name, shown to anyone who enters that crew's join code. Initials are three letters A to Z, and the server refuses a list of offensive combinations. Usernames (letters, numbers and underscore) and crew names (free text up to 48 characters) are not run through a word filter; report one to privacy@trailer-load.com and F-Keys will remove it.
  • Designed for students approximately 14 and older. LOCK IN is not directed to children under 13. Students under 13 should play as guests and not create accounts.
Training supplement · not a certification. LOCK IN builds intuition and tracks practice progress. It is not a certification, credential, or license, and is not a substitute for employer safety training, OSHA training, powered-industrial-truck or equipment certification, or HAZMAT / 49 CFR training.
